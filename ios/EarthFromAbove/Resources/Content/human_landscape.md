# Reading the human landscape

## Irrigation circles

The green circles across the dry West are center-pivot irrigation: a pipe on wheels, up to a quarter mile long, pivots around a well and sprinkles as it goes. A full circle covers about 125 acres. Half and three-quarter circles mark pivots blocked by roads or property lines. Where the groundwater runs out, as it is doing over parts of the Ogallala Aquifer, circles fade back to dryland strips.

## Strip farming and contour ploughing

Alternating strips of crop and fallow, aligned across the wind, are dryland wheat country from Montana to Texas. Curved stripes following the shape of hills are contour ploughing to stop erosion. Both are responses to the Dust Bowl of the 1930s.

## Feedlots and dairies

A rectangular patch of bare brown ground divided into pens, often with a lagoon beside it, near a grain elevator and a railway, is a cattle feedlot. Some hold 100,000 animals. From the air they look like a dusty town with no buildings.

## Mines

Open-pit mines are terraced bowls, with a pale waste dump nearby and often a bright turquoise or rust-coloured pond (the tailings). Strip mines for coal run as long parallel ridges and trenches that move across the land. Gravel pits are small blue-green lakes beside rivers and highways.

## Oil and gas

Oil fields are a fine grid of pale squares (well pads) joined by dirt roads across scrub, with occasional flares at night. Refineries are dense clusters of silver tanks, towers and pipes by a river or coast. Tank farms are rows of white circles.

## Power

- Wind farms: white specks in lines on ridges or grids across fields.
- Solar farms: dark rectangles, sometimes flashing.
- Nuclear plants: a domed or boxy building with one or two enormous cooling towers plumed with steam, by a lake or river.
- Coal and gas plants: tall thin chimneys, a coal pile, and a cooling pond.
- Hydroelectric dams: a straight wall across a river with a long reservoir behind and a tailrace below.

## Ports and shipping

Container ports are rows of giant cranes beside rectangular stacks of containers in bright colours. Ships at anchor outside a port wait in neat rows. Each ship trails a wake; the length of the wake tells you how fast it is going.

## Cities

A city's shape tells its history. A grid aligned north-south is a planned city on the survey system. Radiating avenues mark a capital laid out in the European style, like Washington. Streets that bend with the land are older or grew along rivers and ridges. Suburbs show as curling cul-de-sacs; industrial zones as big flat roofs near railways; the downtown as a cluster of tall shadows.

## Airports

Runways are the longest straight lines in a city, aligned with the prevailing wind. Big airports have parallel pairs. The ends of the runways are marked by the dark rubber of landings.

## Logging and fire

Clear-cuts are pale rectangles or odd polygons in dark forest, often with a lacework of logging roads. Replanted cuts are a slightly lighter green. Fire scars are grey or black with no straight edges, following the terrain and the wind.
