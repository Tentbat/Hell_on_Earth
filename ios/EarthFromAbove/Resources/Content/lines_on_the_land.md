# Lines on the land

## Borders you can and cannot see

Most borders are invisible from the air: the land does not care. But some show up because the rules change at the line.

- The United States-Mexico border through the Imperial Valley is a dead-straight edge between green irrigated fields and brown desert.
- The US-Canada border runs through forest as a cleared strip 6 metres wide, kept open by both countries. From cruise altitude it is a faint straight line through the trees.
- State lines show where farm policy, water rights or land survey systems differ. The Nebraska-Colorado line often separates irrigated circles from dryland strips.
- Where a river is the border, as between Washington and Oregon along the Columbia, you are looking at the border itself.

## Why the Midwest looks like graph paper

In 1785 the United States began surveying new land into a grid of six-mile squares (townships), each cut into 36 one-mile sections. Roads were built along section lines, so from Ohio to the Rockies the land is a lattice of roads one mile apart, and farms are rectangles of 160 acres or their fractions. The grid jogs every 24 miles to correct for the Earth's curvature; look for roads with a sudden kink.

The grid stops where land was settled before 1785 or under Spanish and French rules: along the Mississippi near New Orleans and St. Louis, and in Texas, where long thin "arpent" lots run back from rivers so every farm got water.

## Rivers

Rivers are the easiest lines to read. A river with big loops is on a flat plain; a river in a straight dark slot is in a canyon; a river splitting into frayed strands is carrying gravel from mountains. Oxbow lakes, the crescent-shaped ponds beside a river, are loops it abandoned. Levees show as pale straight lines along the banks.

## The Continental Divide

The Divide runs along the Rockies from Canada to Mexico. Rain on one side flows to the Pacific; on the other to the Atlantic or the Gulf of Mexico. It is not the highest ridge; it is the ridge that separates drainage. In southern Wyoming it splits around the Great Divide Basin, a bowl that drains to no ocean at all.

There are other divides. The Laurentian Divide separates water flowing to Hudson Bay from water flowing to the Gulf of Mexico. In Minnesota a drop of rain can go three ways within a few miles: north to Hudson Bay, east to the Atlantic by the Great Lakes, or south to the Gulf.

## Time zones

Time-zone boundaries follow state lines, county lines and sometimes rivers. Nothing marks them on the ground, but the app announces them so you know when your watch is wrong.

## Other surveyed lines

- The Mason-Dixon line between Pennsylvania and Maryland, surveyed in the 1760s with stones every mile.
- The 49th parallel, 1,200 miles straight across the continent.
- The Four Corners, where four states meet at one point.
- Railways: a dead-straight line across country with gentle curves and no junctions is almost always a railway, often with a town every ten miles where steam engines took on water.
- Pipelines and power lines: straight cleared strips over hills that roads would avoid.
