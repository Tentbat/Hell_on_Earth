# How to be a window spotter

## The horizon is far, far away

When the plane is high up, the horizon (the line where the sky meets the land) is more than 200 miles away. Things near the horizon look tiny and misty. Things in the bottom of your window are close and you can see details.

## Shadows make things pop

Early in the morning and late in the day, everything throws a shadow and the land looks bumpy and real. In the middle of the day it looks flat. If it is early or late, look extra hard: this is the best time.

## Cup your hands

Put your hand against the window like a tunnel and look through. The outside gets brighter. Try it!

## Nature or people?

- Straight lines are almost always made by people: roads, fields, canals.
- Perfect circles are made by people too: they are giant round fields watered by a spinning sprinkler.
- Wiggly lines that split and join are rivers. Nature loves wiggles.

## Is it a dam?

A lake with one straight edge is a lake people made with a dam. It often has long skinny arms where the old valleys were.

## Spot the windmills

Wind turbines look like tiny white toothpicks standing in rows on hills. Solar farms look like dark rectangles that sometimes flash in the sun.

## Plane trails

Those white lines in the sky are made by other planes. A sharp, thin line is brand new. A fuzzy, wide one is old.
